Research

How do people adapt, decide, and work with intelligent systems?

I connect human factors and engineering psychology with the design of training, decision support, and human-centered technologies. My work examines how people assess capabilities, respond to changing demands, and coordinate cognitive work across human and AI agents.

01 / Cognition under pressure

Adaptive Decision Making

I study how people regulate their decision strategies as cognitive resources and situational demands change, and how training can strengthen that adaptation.

  • Cognitive task analysis
  • Simulation experiments
  • Metacognitive training
Matching strategies to cognitive resources

Using the Contextual Control Model in an air traffic control simulation, I examined how available cognitive resources and experience shape strategy selection.

Finding

Performance was highest when decision strategies aligned with available cognitive resources. Experience strengthened participants' ability to select strategies suited to those resources.

Human Factors, 2026

Training pilots for deteriorating weather

Interviews and cognitive task analysis with pilots identified the cues and conditions that trigger strategy changes. I used these insights to develop training that helps pilots monitor their cognitive resources and adapt their decisions.

Study

In an experiment with 40 licensed pilots, trained participants made more protective decisions as weather deteriorated and showed better strategy-resource alignment. The evaluation manuscript is under review.

02 / Shared cognitive work

Human-AI Cognition and Coordination

I investigate how people learn to work with AI teammates, distribute tasks, and combine information to make better collective decisions.

  • Teamwork interventions
  • Group decision tasks
  • Behavioral experiments
Developing human-AI teamwork skills

I developed and evaluated a task-independent intervention grounded in human-human teamwork principles. Participants practiced evaluating goals, developing strategies, allocating tasks, and communicating with an AI teammate.

Finding

Trained participants developed strategies with AI more frequently, delegated more tasks, and completed tasks more efficiently. Their teams also maintained better performance as task difficulty increased.

Journal of Cognitive Engineering and Decision Making, in press

Pooling information in mixed human-AI groups Ongoing

My postdoctoral research at Purdue University uses hidden-profile tasks, in which no member initially holds all the information needed for a group decision.

Research question

When do human-AI groups surface uniquely held information, integrate conflicting evidence, respond to disagreement, and revise initial judgments?

I investigate these processes by examining AI reliability, information access, expressed confidence, and interaction behavior.

03 / Human-centered technologies

Human Performance and Training

I design and evaluate technologies around people's cognitive, perceptual, and physical capabilities, from instructor-led XR authoring to assistive systems.

  • Human-centered design
  • Usability evaluation
  • Human performance measurement
Augmented reality for aviation weather education

In FAA-funded research, I worked with flight instructors to identify training requirements, design an AR authoring toolkit, and evaluate its usability and educational effectiveness.

Contribution

The toolkit enables instructors without programming expertise to create and modify immersive weather scenarios while retaining control over instructional content. Evaluation incorporated perspectives from instructors, experts, and students.

Training requirementsAuthoring toolkitEducational evaluation

Perceptual and physical demands of technology

My studies examine cybersickness and motion cues in flight simulators, as well as the effects of a passive back-support exosuit during sustained posture maintenance and concurrent cognitive activity.

Finding and ongoing work

Exosuit use reduced muscular fatigue and perceived workload while improving performance on a higher-order information-integration task. I also develop measures of educators' readiness to create XR content and perceived display compellingness.

Looking ahead

Future Directions

Adaptive Control and Calibration

I plan to study when people rely on AI, seek information, intervene, or delegate as relative capabilities change. This work distinguishes capability miscalibration from failures to update strategies, and examines how personal goals and social considerations shape AI use. AI-supported public electric vehicle charging provides an initial setting for these questions.

Collective Cognition

I aim to identify when AI teammates help groups share unique evidence, reconsider initial judgments, and reduce cognitive bias. Planned studies examine information distribution, communication, disagreement, and complementary human and AI vulnerabilities.
